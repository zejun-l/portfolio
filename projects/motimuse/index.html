About

MotiMuse is a motivational music practice tool designed by a cross-functional team of five over the course of six weeks for the U-M Ross Business+Tech Innovation Jam. Our design won 1st place in the EdTech track and 2nd overall out of 72 teams, receiving a total of $4,500 in seed funding.

The problem

How can we motivate students to practice their instruments?

50% of children and students aged 10–17 quit their musical activities, with most leaving between ages 15 and 17 (Ruth & Müllensiefen, 2021). This loss matters beyond music: studies consistently show that students who play an instrument outperform their peers in subjects like science, English, and math. Musical participation is also linked to lower anxiety levels, higher conscientiousness, and improved memory. In addition, music has long been central to human connection and community-building. Addressing declining motivation in young musicians is therefore critical, not only for individual development, but for sustaining stronger, more connected communities.

Solution

Gamifying music practice

To address this issue, we designed a music motivation app that makes practice fun and rewards consistency and progress.

Discovery

What are the pain points?

To better understand what factors prevent students from continuing to play an instrument beyond adolescence, we interviewed 5 students aged 7-17 who play an instrument.

After conducting these user interviews, we conducted a thematic analysis using affinity mapping to uncover the patterns and themes to help drive our design decisions

Our user research revealed three main insights that directly informed our design decisions:

1

Inconsistent Practice

"I'll practice it [the flute] whenever I have free time. I don't really have, like, a schedule." - P2

2

Motivation Barriers

"Oh, it's [practicing the violin] kind of a task, I'm not really motivated to do it." - P1

3

Accountability Challenges

"Most of the time, my parents have to remind me to practice" - P3

Competitive analysis

We also conducted a competitive analysis to evaluate competitors' products to identify strengths, weaknesses, and market gaps MotiMuse could fill. While many apps feature gamified characters, streaks, and practice verification, none offer in-game currency or classroom and parental management tools, giving MotiMuse a clear opportunity to provide these unique features.

Wireframing

Early sketches

After the discovery stage, it was time to begin the wireframing process by sketching out a very rough task flow to show possible user interactions in the app.

Low-fidelity wireframes

Building on the sketches, I created low-fidelity wireframes showing the key screens and features of the app.

Final designs

Main features

After multiple iterations and incorporating feedback from my team and the competition judges, I finalized my design with high-fidelity wireframes and created a clickable prototype.

Building Consistency

The practice tracker helps users visualize their daily practice time. Each day that they practice, they build their streak, reminding them that they’re building momentum and making progress.

Making Practice Fun

Users choose a customizable avatar that levels up with their progress, turning music practice into a gamified experience

Fostering Motivation

As users practice, they earn in-game currency that can be spent in the shop to buy new outfits and accessories for their avatar.

Ensuring Accountability

The AI listening feature ensures the user is actually practicing and not using a recording or playing random notes.

Additional features

We also designed additional features provide more flexibility and support for different users.

Regulating tech use

The optional time limit feature allows parents to limit the amount of time their kids spend on the MotiMuse, as the app isn’t meant to be an addiction; it’s meant to serve as a foundation for healthy practice habits..
