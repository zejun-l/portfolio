About

This project centered on a redesign of the interface of U-M GPT, the University of Michigan's custom generative AI platform, including the implementation of a new feature to improve the user experience of students. Completed within a semester-long intro to UX course, this project progressed through the full UX design process, starting with the client brief and initial sketches to low- and high-fidelity wireframes, all underscored by an emphasis on strong storytelling and thoughtful design choices.

The problem

How can we increase student use?

U-M GPT is the University of Michigan's custom generative AI platform that supports multiple commercial and open-source language models and AI art generators. It can do everything ChatGPT can do, but the key differences are that 1) U-M GPT is completely free for Michigan students, staff, and faculty, and 2) it's private, meaning nobody reviews the prompts you enter and your prompts aren't sold to third parties or used to train the AI.

While this sounds like a great tool for the U-M community, many people, especially students, don't know it exists or understand why they should use it over chatbots like ChatGPT. This project aimed to redesign U-M GPT to increase student engagement by making it more modern, familiar, and user-friendly, aligning its interface and experience with common mental models and UX best practices from popular chatbots.

Wireframing

Early sketches

To start my wireframing process, I sketched out 8 different ideas of a possible new feature that could enhance U-M GPT and built on one of them by sketching out a low-fidelity task flow.

I chose to build on the split-screen view feature and created a task flow to show possible user interactions.

Low-fidelity wireframes

Building on my sketches and task flow, I created low-fidelity wireframes for the three ideas I thought would be the most useful for both mobile and desktop.

The next step was to pick one idea and design 6 pages in both mobile and desktop using Autolayout.

Originally, I wanted to expand on my split-screen view idea; however, based on feedback I received that this feature risked placing cognitive overload on users as they try to juggle multiple chats at one time, I decided to go with the bookmark feature, as I wanted to keep my design accessible and not overwhelming.

The idea behind the bookmark feature is to provide students (who may be using generative AI for multiple classes, projects, etc.) with a way to easily group and return to important chats without losing track of where specific information was discussed.

At this point, I also began to finalize how I wanted the finished interface to look. I created various components and revised the design for a more polished look.

Usability testing

Through conducting a quick usability test with a classmate using my low-fidelity prototype, I received feedback that resulted in some changes to provide users with a more seamless experience. One of these changes included allowing users to more quickly and easily access bookmark folders from the sidebar when using U-M GPT on a desktop.

Final designs

High-fidelity wireframes

With the feedback from the usability testing in mind, I finalized my design with high-fidelity wireframes and created a clickable prototype.

During this step, I also designed a landing page to introduce the various generative AI tools and services the University of Michigan offers, as well as highlight their unique features. First impressions are impactful, and designing a landing page that tells a story increases user understanding and engagement.

Wrapping it up

This was my first solo design project, completed while I was learning Figma for the first time. Learning and designing simultaneously made the process especially rewarding and helped me build confidence in both the tool and my design approach. My biggest takeaway was understanding that iteration drives strong design, and that meaningful improvements often happen after the first version.
