About

This project was completed as an assignment for the class Games & UX, where we explored the industry practice of video games user research and design. Our team identified areas for improvement and proposed design changes for a hypothetical remake/update of the game Stardew Valley based on insights from market research and user interviews.

The problem

How can we improve co-op play?

A cozy country life RPG, Stardew Valley has become an extremely popular game in recent years. Co-op play, in particular, is highly enjoyed by players who want to share a farm with their friends. While this mode of gameplay provides players with a fun, collaborative environment, it also introduces unique challenges. Across online communities and through our own experiences, our team observed recurring frustrations specific to co-op gameplay mechanics. As a result, we wanted to better understand these pain points and provide solutions for an improved co-op play experience.

Data synthesis

Thematic analysis

To better understand player experiences in Stardew Valley’s multiplayer mode and uncover opportunities to design features that enhance customization and collaboration, we interviewed 7 players with co-op play experience. The transcripts from these user interviews were then used to conduct a thematic analysis; the method we chose was affinity diagramming.

First, we reviewed each user interview transcript and highlighted text that was related to our research question, like pain points, values, and experiences.

Next, we grouped similar highlighted segments and assigned each cluster a code, a phrase that acted as a label for the data. This helped us organize and synthesize user insights by identifying patterns in the co-op player experience.

Our thematic analysis revealed a range of user sentiments; in particular, two key insights that offered clear opportunities for improvement.

Design updates

Item transfer solutions

We proposed two game design updates to make the item transfer experience more user-friendly in different scenarios.

Near another player

Players found sharing resources with a nearby teammate to be slow and tedious, as thrown items were often picked right back up by the giver due to the game's gravity physics. To solve this, we designed a feature where right-clicking on a nearby player opens a 3x3 grid inventory, allowing for quick, multi-item transfers and streamlining collaboration.

  1. Right-click on another player
  2. 3x3 grid opens to send items
  3. Gift animation for item transfer confirmation

Across the map

Having to travel across the map to deliver items wasted valuable playtime for players. To address this, we proposed a homing pigeon mechanic that lets players send a single item remotely. The pigeon physically travels across the map, offering a faster (not instantaneous) method for transferring items, preserving game balance while improving convenience.

  1. Buy a pet license to recieve the pigeon and whistle
  2. Call the pigeon with the whistle to send items

Timing issue solutions

To address the issue of players finding frustration with game timing in co-op mode, we proposed two solutions to give players more control over their gameplay experience.

Day length setting

In co-op, players felt rushed and struggled to complete tasks because time kept moving during conversations and while they were in menus — unlike in single-player, where it pauses. To address this, we suggested adding a setting to adjust the length of an in-game day, giving players more control, helping recover lost time, and reducing pressure.

Universal pause

Players have no way to pause the game for everyone in multiplayer. This is an issue when players need to step away or face technical issues and miss in-game opportunities. We proposed a universal pause system where any player can request a pause, and if all agree, the game halts — making co-op mode more flexible and user-friendly.
